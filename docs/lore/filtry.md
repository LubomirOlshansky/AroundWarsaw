# Stacja Filtrów: lore notes

Source: Polish Wikipedia, "Zespół Stacji Filtrów w Warszawie". For cards, radio lines, missions.

- Designed by William Lindley with his son William Heerlein Lindley. Built 1883-1886 under mayor Sokrat Starynkiewicz, paid for mostly with city bonds. First clean water on 3 July 1886.
- About 36 ha between Koszykowa, Krzywickiego, Filtrowa and Raszyńska.
- 36 slow filters in six groups (82,236 m²) under the lawns, four covered settling tanks, nine clear-water reservoirs with brick vaults on granite piers.
- Water tower, 1885-86, out of regular use since 1924. Rapid Filter Plant 1929-33 under a Canadian licence.
- Bust of Starynkiewicz near the tower, 1907. His multi-stemmed yew beside it is a monument of nature.
- 1915: the retreating Russians blew up the Kierbedź Bridge and cut Praga's pipes, supply back on 30 September.
- The Mermaid on Wybrzeże Kościuszkowskie: Ludwika Nitschowa made it in a disused boiler hall here, set up in 1939.
- 1939: bombing, water cut on 24 September, back on 4 October at about 20%.
- 1944: the Germans held the site, used the beds as stables, blew up the River Pumping Station on 18 September, and the last workers left on 22 September. Water back on 29 May 1945.
- 1952: pre-war capacity of 180,000 m³ a day again. 2008-10: the ozone station, named "Socrates" after a contest.
- 2012: declared a historic monument. It still supplies Warsaw today, and the Museum of Waterworks stands in Technical Building No. 2.
- Film: in *Czterdziestolatek* Madzia Karwowska tested water in a lab at the Rapid Filter Plant.
